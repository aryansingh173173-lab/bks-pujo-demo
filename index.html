The occasion

There is no better week to be present in Bengal.

Durga Puja is on the UNESCO list of Intangible Cultural Heritage. A study led by Queen Mary University of London with the British Council, supported by IIT Kharagpur, valued the creative economy around the festival at ₹32,377 crore — roughly 2.58 per cent of West Bengal’s annual GDP, generated inside a single week.

Those are their figures, not ours, and we cite them for one reason. This is the week the state stops and looks. Attention in Bengal in October is not available at any other price, in any other month.

What we are doing inside that week is narrow and specific. One pandal, on the ground of a working fishery and farming committee in the East Kolkata Wetlands, built around the person who grows the food — with a live integrated farm beside it that visitors walk through rather than read about.

What you would actually get

Four kinds of people, one piece of ground, one week.

Most festival sponsorship sells you a surface — a board, a banner, a logo on an arch. This is a position rather than a surface, so it is worth being plain about who it stands you next to.

01

The public, at close range

Kolkata walks pandals for a week. People arrive on their own time, with their families, in a good mood, willing to stop and look at things properly. Very little else in the year puts a company in front of that many people in that frame of mind.

02

The growers whose ground this is

The Puja stands on Munshir Bheri, held by a working fishery and farming committee. They are hosts here, not an audience assembled for a campaign. If you sell to smallholders, you are standing on their land as their guest.

03

The people we bring

Farmer families, karyakartas and partners from the KarmYog network come to this Puja. Exactly who is invited, from where, and how your team meets them is something we work out with you — not something we assert on a website.

04

Institutions and press, informally

Government, research and media people walk pandals like everybody else. We will not promise you a meeting or a headline. We will say that October in Kolkata is when doors that stay shut in June get propped open.

The working farm

Your product on a real smallholding, not on a slide.

On the same ground as the pandal we are building a live integrated farm: crop, pond, livestock and market held together on one holding, the way a small West Bengal family actually farms, so what one part throws away becomes what the next part needs.

For a technology, input or finance business this is the part that is genuinely hard to buy anywhere else. Monitoring, drip, pond energy, soil and water diagnostics, advisory, credit — whatever you sell into a smallholding can be installed, run and explained on a holding the size your customers actually farm, in front of the people who decide, during the week they are all present.

We are open about what works and what does not, and we will not describe a pilot as a rollout.

  • Equipment running on a real holding, not standing in a demonstration booth.
  • Growers who can ask your team questions in Bangla, at their own pace, without an appointment.
  • Your field staff and channel partners on the ground with you, doing the explaining themselves.
  • Somewhere to bring your buyers, your board and your investors in October.

We do not sell data, we do not resell farmer records, and no partnership includes access to personal information about farmers.

The honest part

What we will put in writing, and what we will never claim.

You will be shown other festival proposals this season, and most of them will lead with a number. Here is the trade we are offering instead.

We will commit, in writing

  • The dates, the venue and the ground.
  • Where your name and your mark appear, at what size, on what.
  • The space you hold on the demonstration farm, and what you may install in it.
  • What your team may do on site, and when.
  • Named people on our side who are accountable for each of those.
  • Where your contribution goes, and the village farms it seeds.

We will never claim

  • A footfall figure. No Puja can verify one, and the numbers quoted to you elsewhere are estimates dressed as counts.
  • Media impressions, reach or coverage.
  • A return on your spend.
  • Government partnership, endorsement or any scheme.
  • Access to farmer data, which belongs to the farmer and the programme and is not ours to trade.
  • A yield claim about the farm, or a metric about the platform.

You will find sponsorships that promise you all six. We would rather be the one your finance director can check.

After October

A festival ends. A system does not.

The Pujo is coordinated on OmniDEL.ai, a work platform KarmYog built and owns, with an assistant called MahAcharyaJi inside it. The team works with the Acharya in Bangla, Hindi or English, mostly by voice, because they are standing in a field or on a build site rather than sitting at a desk.

The reason this should matter to you is not that we wrote software. You may well have written better. It matters because the same farm records, task cycles and Acharya conversations that run the festival in October are what carry the village farm programme through the following year. The pandal comes down in November. The records do not.

So this is not a week that closes on the last night and leaves you with photographs. And if your own business already runs on farmer records and advisory, you will find we speak the same operational language before the first meeting starts.

A

Building the Pujo

Site planning, vendors, budgets, permissions, volunteer rosters and daily field reports in one place. Every karyakarta sees the tasks that belong to them and marks them off from the field, and the picture updates for everyone at once. Nothing here runs out of a spreadsheet one person owns.

B

Working with farmers

Each integrated farm has a record. The crop, pond, livestock and market cycles turn into scheduled tasks and reminders in the farmer’s own language, by voice where reading is a barrier. Farmers ask the Acharya practical questions and get an answer at the hour they are actually working.

C

Learning through the year

Integrated Farming only works if a farmer learns a system rather than a single crop. The Acharya carries that teaching in small pieces tied to the week’s work, rather than as a training day forgotten by the next season. Records make costs visible, and visible numbers make the next decision better.

See it working

The platform is not a prototype and not a render. It is at omnidel.ai, it is in the hands of our karyakartas today, and we will walk your team through the live system in the sponsorship conversation.

The contribution

What sponsorship pays for

Two things at once: the public week in October, and the village farms the week exists to seed. One village integrated farm is seeded at ₹1 lakh — payable in full, or as ₹20,000 every two months across the year.

Five thousand farms is the goal, and ₹50 crore is simply what five thousand farms costs. Every seeded farm comes onto OmniDEL.ai with the money: the record, the task cycle and the Acharya in the farmer’s own language are part of the seed, not an extra line on an invoice.

What is Bharatiya Krishak Samaj Pujo?
A Durga Puja that puts the farmer inside the gathering, shows Integrated Farming as a working livelihood, and invites neighbours, supporters and partners to take part. Organised by KarmYog for the 21st Century, with Bharatiya Krishak Samaj as organising partner.
Where exactly is it?
Munshir Bheri Management / Fishermen’s Committee, near Sukantanagar and Salt Lake Sector V, in the East Kolkata Wetlands, Kolkata – 700091, West Bengal. The demonstration farm and the Puja share the same ground.
When is it?
16–20 October 2026.
Why is farming in a Puja?
Because the Puja is when Bengal gathers. This gathering names the farmer, recognises the farmers nobody photographs, and then shows Integrated Farming so that livelihood is something visitors walk through rather than read on a board.
What is Integrated Farming?
Crop, animals, water and market held together on one holding, so the waste from one part becomes the input for the next. On a small West Bengal holding that is the difference between one income and several.
What is OmniDEL.ai?
The work platform this whole project runs on. KarmYog for the 21st Century built it and owns it. Tasks, teams, sites, vendors, farm records and field reporting all sit in it, and an AI assistant called MahAcharyaJi sits inside it. It is at omnidel.ai and it is in daily use.
What does the AI actually do?
Two jobs. For the team it turns plans into tasks, keeps the field and the office looking at the same picture, and takes reports from people who are standing outdoors. For farmers it answers practical questions in their own language, carries the crop, pond and livestock calendar as reminders, and keeps a record the farmer can use to make the next decision.
Which languages does it work in?
Bangla, Hindi and English, spoken or typed. Voice matters here: many of the people using it are working with their hands, and some read less comfortably than they speak.
Is the AI a gimmick for the festival?
No. It is how the Pujo is being built. Remove it and the project does not run. It is also the reason the platform does not end in October — the same records and task cycles carry the village farm programme through the year.
