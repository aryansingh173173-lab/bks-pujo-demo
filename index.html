Durga Puja 2026 · Kolkata · Founding partnerships open

Bengal's oldest Puja ritual is a farming ritual.This year, the farmer steps out of the ritual and into the pandal.

On the seventh morning of Durga Puja, before the crowds arrive, priests carry a bundle of nine plants to the river and bathe it at first light. Banana, turmeric, pomegranate, wood apple, arum, colocasia, ashoka, jayanti and paddy. The bundle is wrapped in a red bordered sari and set beside Ganesha, and for the rest of the week Bengal worships it as the goddess herself. The paddy is Lakshmi. The turmeric is Durga.

Long before it became the largest public festival in India, this was a harvest rite, cultivators asking autumn for a good crop. That thread was never cut. It was only forgotten.

In 2026 we pick it up again. One pandal in the East Kolkata Wetlands, built around the women and men who grow the food, with a working integrated farm on the same ground that visitors walk through. This is the first year. The companies who come in now are the founding names on it.

The festival

A religious festival and a civic one at the same time. There is nothing else quite like it.

Durga Puja is the only Indian festival on the UNESCO list of Intangible Cultural Heritage, and it was listed not for the worship alone but for everything the city builds around it: the artistry, the craft economy, and the way an entire population comes out of doors for a week to look at what its neighbours have made.

At heart it is a homecoming. Durga returns to her father's house with her children, stays a few days, and is walked to the river. Bengal grieves a little every year when she goes. That is the emotional weather of the whole week, and it is why nobody here treats Puja as a holiday. It is a return.

It is also the largest open air exhibition in the world that nobody calls an exhibition. The sculptors of Kumartuli work through the monsoon. Pandals are commissioned like architecture, argued about like cinema, and queued for like concerts, with families walking from midnight until four in the morning to see the ones they have heard about.

A study led by Queen Mary University of London with the British Council, supported by IIT Kharagpur, valued the creative economy around the festival at ₹32,377 crore, roughly 2.58 per cent of West Bengal's annual GDP, generated in a single week.

The figure is not the interesting part. This is.

It is the only place in India where people queue, at night, in the rain, to get into something you have put your name on.

The idea

One pandal. One subject. The hands that grow the food.

Bengal's pandals are arguments. Each year a handful of them say something the state then discusses for months, about migration, women's safety, the river or the climate.

Ours says something older and simpler.

The nine plants are already in the ritual. The paddy is already the goddess. What is missing is the person who put the paddy in the ground: the woman transplanting in ankle deep water in July, the man walking his pond bank at dawn, the family that decides in June what everyone eats in November.

So the pandal is built around their year. Their water, their soil, their tools, their calendar. Not a photograph of a farmer at the entrance. The whole structure.

And beside it, on the same ground, a farm that genuinely works: crop, pond, livestock and market held together on one holding, the way a small Bengal family really farms, where what one part throws away is what the next part needs. Nobody will read about integrated farming on a signboard. They will walk through it.

What is being built

So there is no ambiguity about what your name goes on.

Five things exist by 16 October 2026, and every one of them is somewhere a partner can stand.

01

The pandal

A full Durga Puja pandal in the East Kolkata Wetlands, designed around the farming year, built by Bengal karigars, and open to the public across the festival.

02

The demonstration farm

A working integrated farm on the same ground, with paddy, pond, livestock, vegetables, composting and water management arranged as one walkable cycle.

03

The technology plots

Defined spaces inside the farm where companies install and run their own equipment on a real smallholding, staffed by their own people, in front of growers.

04

The programme

Five days of aarti, dhaak, food, craft and farmer conversations on the ground, including growers from the wetlands themselves, who host this.

05

The record

The whole build and the whole week, filmed and photographed properly, from the first bamboo to the immersion.

Who is behind it

Two organisations. One organises it. One gives it its purpose. Neither pays the other.

The roles are separate and specific, so a partner knows exactly who is accountable.

Organiser

KarmYog for the 21st Century Foundation

KarmYog conceives, builds and runs the Pujo. The ground, the pandal, the farm, the programme, the safety and the money all sit with KarmYog. Your agreement is with KarmYog and with nobody else. The Foundation also organised Durga Puja Mahotsav 2025 at the IIT Kharagpur Research Park.

Organisation Partner

Bharatiya Krishak Samaj

Bharatiya Krishak Samaj is a farmers' organisation with a single preoccupation: the upliftment of the farmer. That is the entire reason this partnership exists, and it is why the Pujo carries their name.

No money moves between us. Bharatiya Krishak Samaj is not paid to lend its name and takes no share of sponsorship. It is not a sponsor, it does not hold the title position, and it is not a commercial party to anything on this page.

Why that should matter to you

The farming subject here is not a theme invented for a festival. The organisation that does this work every day of the year put its name to it before a single sponsor was approached. When your brand stands next to the farmer at this Pujo, it is standing next to something that was already true.

Why a company would want this

The rarest thing in this sector is a public square.

You already talk to farmers every day, in an app, on a call, at a centre or in a field. What you rarely get is somewhere thousands of people, including the ones you serve, can see what you stand for all at once, unhurried, without you buying their attention.

That is what a Puja is. And this one is about your subject.

01

Show the work, not the description

An advisory tool, sensor, irrigation line, credit product or buying platform can run here on a real holding, explained by your own people in Bangla to growers who will ask hard questions.

02

Bengal is wide open

There are millions of smallholder families here, an enormous rice and fish economy, and comparatively little noise. Your rupee travels further in Kolkata in October than almost anywhere else in India.

03

Your own people are already here

Your field officers, dealers, FPO partners and their families are going to a Puja that week whatever you do. Making it yours turns a week of leave into the proudest week of their year.

04

This is year one

The film

One week in October. A year of material.

Between the first bamboo and the immersion, this happens in front of a camera.

Karigars bending frames by hand in the wet heat of August. The farm going into the ground. Water going into the pond. Growers arriving on Shashthi and finding themselves the subject of the thing. The dhaak starting. Dhunuchi smoke. The queue at two in the morning. Faces of people looking at something about their own lives and recognising it. And on Dashami, all of it walked to the river.

We are documenting the whole build and the whole week.

The Title Partner has first call on the footage and stills for its own campaigns, and part of the shoot runs to its brief. The Creator and Broadcast Partner is credited across the creator films and the highlight cut. Every partner gets the material covering its own presence.

What it becomes

A harvest season film, a Republic Day post, a recruitment video and a year of social, out of one week in October.

Where the money goes

Sponsorship pays for the Pujo. Nothing else.

Partner contributions fund the event and only the event: the pandal and its build, the karigars, the demonstration farm and its planting, the technology plots, the five days of programming, safety and crowd management, and the documentation.

We will show you the line items.

It is not a donation

It is not CSR dressed up. It is sponsorship of a cultural event, and we would rather it sat in your brand budget where it belongs.

It does not fund village farms

KarmYog's village farm programme, seeding integrated farms at ₹1 lakh each, is real and separately funded. No part of sponsorship goes into it, and none is being asked of you.

A different conversation

If you would like to support the village programme, it is a different conversation on a different day, and it carries no visibility.

Questions

The practical questions, answered plainly.

Commercial first, then the organisations, the ground and the time involved.

What exactly does our name appear on?
It depends on the partnership. For the Title Partner: the pandal, the arch, the invitation, the farm gate and all printed and digital material. For the others, the specific placements are written into the agreement before you commit.
Who is organising this, KarmYog or Bharatiya Krishak Samaj?
KarmYog for the 21st Century Foundation organises the Pujo, runs the ground and holds the money. Your agreement is with KarmYog. Bharatiya Krishak Samaj is the Organisation Partner and the Pujo carries its name because that organisation exists to uplift farmers. It is a partnership of purpose, not a commercial arrangement. No money passes between the two, and Bharatiya Krishak Samaj is not a sponsor and does not hold the title position.
Is Bharatiya Krishak Samaj being paid for the use of its name?
No. Nothing is paid to them and they take no share of any sponsorship. They are partners because the cause is ours.
Can a competitor be here too?
Not in your category, and not in your technology domain. That is the point of the structure, and it is contractual.
Is a religious festival right for a national brand?
Durga Puja is a religious festival and a civic one at once, which is unusual and is exactly why UNESCO listed it. In Kolkata everybody visits pandals, across faiths and with no faith at all. Corporate sponsorship of Durga Puja has been ordinary here for decades. Your name sits with the farming story and the pandal, not with the liturgy. There is no political affiliation of any kind.
Does our money go to the village farms?
No. Sponsorship funds the Pujo, including the pandal, the farm on the ground, the programme and the documentation. KarmYog's village farm programme is separate and separately funded.
Who else is partnering?
We will tell you who has committed and who is in conversation, by name, on a call. We do not put companies on a website before they have signed, and we would extend you the same courtesy.
What if it is badly attended, or badly built?
Come and see the ground and meet the team before you commit anything. The same organisers ran Durga Puja Mahotsav 2025 at the IIT Kharagpur Research Park. Everything is written down, with named people accountable, before money moves.
What do we get that we can still use in March?
The film and photographs, and, if you took a plot, a set of conversations with growers that tell you things a survey will not.
How much of our team's time does this take?
One decision maker, one marketing contact, and two people on the ground during the week if you have a plot.
When do we have to decide?
Before the design locks in August, if you want the title.
What does it cost, and is that the end of it?
Title ₹10 lakh, farm technology ₹3 lakh, category ₹2.5 lakh, creator and broadcast ₹4 lakh, craft and community ₹1.5 lakh, and supporting ₹50,000. All are indicative and agreed in writing first. Nothing further afterwards.
Can we pay through this website?
No. Nothing is taken online. There is no checkout, no booking and no reservation.
What happens after we get in touch?
The form writes a file to your own computer, and this site sends nothing anywhere. You email it to us when you are ready, and a person replies to arrange a time.
